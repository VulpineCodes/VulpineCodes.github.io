PharmaFlow

Reliable data infrastructure for the small pharmacies that big software forgets.

PharmaFlow is a data platform for small pharmacies: idempotent ingestion of inventory and sales records, data-quality quarantine, analytics that run in under a second, event streaming that survives crashes without double-applying stock, and an API serving it all. Every claim in its documentation is backed by a test or a benchmark with its conditions recorded.

Why it exists

My father ran a small pharmacy in Lagos. During COVID I watched what happens when foot traffic disappears, customers avoid in-person visits, and the business still runs on paper ledgers and phone calls to coordinate deliveries: the operation doesn't stop, but it runs blind.

PharmaFlow is the platform I wish he'd had — inventory and operations infrastructure sized for a small pharmacy, engineered with the seriousness usually reserved for much bigger companies. It deliberately stays on the operations side: no patient or prescription data, ever.

My role: Solo designer and engineer — ingestion, storage, analytics, streaming, API, benchmarks.

Architecture

Engineering decisions & trade-offs

Idempotency as a foundation, not a feature

File hashes and row hashes make every import safely replayable — verified across separate processes against the same database file. Re-running yesterday's import is a no-op, not an incident.

Analytics bottleneck, measured then fixed

At 50,000 rows the DuckDB analytics took 61.6 seconds. Profiling showed the SQL took 0.07s — the other ~59s was row-by-row Python inserts. Switching to a bulk appender load brought it to 0.49s (125×), and a correctness gate asserts the new loader returns results identical to the old one.

Exactly-once effect from at-least-once delivery

The streaming layer commits offsets only after processing and dedupes by event ID. In the crash test — consumer killed after applying 2 of 4 events, before committing — restart replays all four and the final stock is exactly right. Poison events go to a dead-letter queue instead of wedging the consumer.

Honest benchmarks or none

Every performance number ships with its conditions: machine, versions, dataset seed, single-run caveats. Numbers measured on a laptop are labeled as such — they are evidence about the code, not marketing about production scale.

Testing & evidence

  • 60 pytest tests passing, including cross-process idempotency, crash-recovery, streaming crash tests, and the analytics correctness gate.
  • dbt executed for real against DuckDB: seeds, models, and all 8 data tests green.
  • Benchmarks at 1k / 10k / 50k rows committed with full conditions in benchmarks/RESULTS.md.

Results

  • Ingestion ≈ 7,000 rows/second at 50k rows; duplicate re-import of 50k rows completes in 0.144s.
  • Analytics at 50k rows: 61.6s → 0.49s after the bulk-load fix, with identical results.
  • Streaming crash test: final inventory exactly-once (90 units, not 80) after mid-batch consumer failure.

Honest limitations

  • The Kafka/Redpanda and PostgreSQL adapters are written and reviewed but unverified — no broker or database server in the local environment; the tested paths are the file-log broker and SQLite store.
  • Terraform is reviewed for consistency but unapplied; Grafana/Prometheus remain Compose declarations.
  • Benchmarks are local single-machine figures, explicitly not production scale. Not deployed.
